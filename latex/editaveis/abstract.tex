\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
  The organization of amateur football matches in Brasília faces recurring challenges, particularly in public courts. Games are usually arranged through dispersed messaging groups, resulting in inconsistent confirmations and little clarity about space availability. In response, this work presents BoraFut, a cross-platform mobile application that supports amateur football organization in the Federal District by connecting players, courts, and local groups. Development followed a hybrid methodological approach combining plan-driven elements and agile practices grounded in Extreme Programming (XP). Requirements elicitation relied on observation, brainwriting, and a questionnaire answered by 64 amateur players, while prioritization adopted the MoSCoW method and hierarchical backlog decomposition. The delivered solution comprises a cross-platform mobile application and a backend service with a relational database, real-time communication, and an automated test suite subject to a coverage gate. Validation was conducted in two stages: five sessions with a high-fidelity prototype, which yielded a raw mean score of 85.5 on the System Usability Scale, and a production field test with 27 recorded arrivals and 15 matches effectively started. The application supported the event without becoming a bottleneck. The results provide initial evidence of positive perceived usability and operational feasibility, subject to the small sample, the authors' participation in the operation, and the need for longitudinal evaluation of recurring adoption.

  \noindent 
  \textbf{Keywords}: amateur football. mobile application. software engineering. agile methodologies. public spaces.
  \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
